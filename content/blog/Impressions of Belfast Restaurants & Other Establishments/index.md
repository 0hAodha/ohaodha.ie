## Introduction
For a period of twelve weeks between September and December of this year, I lived out of a hotel in Belfast for work, with my employer paying for my accommodation and subsistence, with .

For a period of twelve weeks, I will be living in a hotel Belfast for work, and will have the ability to claim expenses for eating out for lunch and dinner.
Having now been in Belfast for three weeks, and having visited a great many restaurants and pubs in the city, I realised that by the end of these twelve weeks I will most likely have a decent insight into these establishments and be able to share recommendations.
I am, however, not qualified to make full reviews of these establishments, nor would I really have the time to do so properly even if I were qualified to do so, so instead I decided that I would make a blog post with some quick summaries of my general impressions of these Belfast establishments. 

The following are my impressions of these establishments, in no particular order (except sometimes chronological) and with no particular claim to objectivity.

### The Dirty Onion
- A pub with a large outdoor area in the Cathedral Quarter, next to the Thirsty Goat.
- No smoking allowed, which I suppose can be taken as a positive or a negative depending on whether you want to be inhaling smoke or not.
- Expensive drinks, again circa £6.50.
- Enjoyable atmosphere and good live music the night that I was there.
- Unpleasant bathrooms, but normal by Belfast standards[^1].
- Decent Guinness; no Beamish or Murphy's.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> wouldn't necessarily recommend, but I'll probably be back. Would recommend against making a habit of buying drinks here.

### White's Tavern
- Showed up on one occasion in a party of four and struggled to get the server's attention to get a menu, despite standing in front of him at the bar and him seemingly not being busy at all.
- We ordered three steaks and a burger, and they made us pay in advance as they suspected us of dine-'n'-dashing.
- For some reason, we made the mistake of returning: the service was even worse than previously, with the server not coming over to our table to take our orders for at least 20 minutes, the food arriving lukewarm at best, and being thoroughly unremarkable (although I had a blocked nose at the time so I can't really comment on flavour).
- Also, on our second visit they had removed the steak and the burger from the menu, leaving only pseudo-"traditional" Irish dishes.
- Nice building & atmosphere, but they exclusively play what can only be described as "Paddy music": not traditional Irish music, but what an American would imagine traditional Irish music to be, cleansed of any and all nationalist or political illusions.
I counted three different pirate songs being played on our last visit, for reasons unknown to me other than perhaps they sounded vaguely Irish: I can understand *What Will We Do with the Drunken Sailor* as it's popular and the same tune is used in many Irish folk songs, but the rest were genuinely just pirate music for LARPers.
- Bizarre pseudo-Irish pub with an interior that feels like a traditional old Irish pub and service & music that feels like a tourist scam.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> neither I nor any member of our party would ever return to that establishment for food. Probably quite nice if you're only drinking though, but I wouldn't return even just for a drink.

- Security guards do seem to be on the ball to be fair.

### Greggs
- Cheap and cheerful.
- Probably the only good thing that Northern Ireland has that the Republic doesn't, except maybe the NHS.
- Great sausage rolls.
- Bad coffee, but not bad for the price.
- Tea is fine but I think you have to specifically request milk (I have ordered tea three different times in different Greggs in different countries and always forget to ask for milk), although it's perfectly drinkable without milk.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> would recommend, a necessary cultural experience and great for a cheap & cheerful bite.

### KFC
- KFC is one of those fast food restaurants that you always imagine to be great despite it being consistently bad and having no memory of ever enjoying it except once as a very young child.
- I did however once walk home through a crowd of teenage boys who were throwing glass bottles and other detritus at two men some distance away for reasons unknown to me (and, I suspect, unknown to both the teenagers and the men) while carrying a large KFC bag and I passed through without the slightest bother, so it is possible that it has some ability to ward off badness, which would explain why the restaurant itself is a far more pleasant environment than the nearby McDonald's.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> KFC is always bad.

### Ragin' Ramen
- Nice food.
- Somewhat expensive by ramen standards, around £16 to £20 for a bowl of ramen. 
- I wouldn't describe the ramen as "authentic"[^2] or up to the standard that I found in Japan, but I think that would be an unfair criticism of a restaurant that advertises itself as "fusion" food.
For example, I got a steak a ramen which came with very thin ("shoestring") fries, in the ramen itself.
- Proper disposable chopsticks on the tables (which is a plus considering I was once handed a knife and fork to eat what I would describe as wildly inauthentic and unpleasant ramen at the Jalan Jalan on Newcastle Road in Galway, although I really ought not to have been surprised).
- Only major criticism is that the music isn't great.
- On one visit, the staff were very accommodating in attempting to slot our party of four in between reservations.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> would certainly recommend, but not for a cheap nor authentic experience.
- wanker waiter

### Pizza Punks
- I was underwhelmed by the pepperoni pizza I had here but it was fine.
I feel that since pizza is so ubiquitously good, for a pizza restaurant to be considered "good" it has to be *really* good.
- The main attraction of this restaurant is the ability to customise your pizza with as many additional toppings as you like with no additional cost, which I didn't do, hence my experience probably isn't representative of the prototypical Pizza Punks experience.
Others whom I was with at this restaurant got everything they could on the pizza and seemed to greatly enjoy it.
- A "discretionary" 10% service charge is included automatically in the bill, which you can request be removed, but I would say that the automatic inclusion makes in tantamount to mandatory.
- <span style="font-variant: small-caps; font-weight: bold">Verdict:</span> I personally wouldn't go back, but possibly good if you enjoy customising pizzas.

## Hotels
I am unusual in that I despise what many consider "good service" in hotels:
I don't want someone coming in my room while I'm not there to clean, I don't want free low-quality toiletries, and I don't want to have to lock all my valuables away whenever I leave because I know someone will be coming into my room while I'm gone.
I don't like making presumably underpaid workers do things for me that I'm perfectly capable of doing myself:
on one occasion a porter at the Yokohama Bay Hotel seemed very surprised and taken aback when I didn't want to give her my suitcase to take up to my room --- I was on the way to my room anyway, why make someone else go out of their way to wheel a suitcase for me that I had already wheeled to the hotel?

To be honest, I am a man on whom nice hotels are wasted.
My ideal hotel is a Premier Inn: all I want is a clean room with a bed and a bathroom, and not to be disturbed.

### The Quarter by the Warren Collection
- Former student accommodation.
- Very thin walls and seemingly frequented by rather amorous holidaymakers; I would describe the noise levels of my neighbours around 21:00 one evening as "conscious" and "deliberate".
- Kitchen areas are decent and reasonably well-provisioned with cooking utensils.
- "Continental breakfast" means a buffet of Tesco's finest pancakes, crumpets, and pain au chocolats, along with some own-brand cereal, but I don't usually eat breakfast anyway so it made no odds to me.
- £5 to use the laundry room (which is actually quite reasonable for giving you both washer and dryer access, considering I was once charged €4 to use a washing machine in a [Brussels hostel](https://www.meininger-hotels.com/en/hotels/brussels/hotel-bruxelles-gare-du-midi/) and another €4 to use the dryer just to have my clothes coming out smelling like stagnant lakewater).
- Nowhere to air-dry clothes, as one would probably expect.
- Limited storage space within the room, difficult to unpack if staying long-term.
- Nowhere to open suitcase besides the bed.
