The standard greeting in Modern Irish is <span class="gaelic">Dia duit</span> (*Dia duit* / *God be with you*), with the standard response being <span class="gaelic">Dia is Muire duit</span> (*Dia is Muire duit* / *God and Mary be with you*);
of course, these greetings are distinctly Christian, and distinctly Catholic in character (as the Catholic religion places a significant emphasis on Marian devotion[^1] which many other denominations do not).
A question which occurs naturally to many, and that is of particular importance to those learners of Modern Irish who wish to avoid any association with Christianity, is the question of what greeting was used in pre-Christian Ireland?

This is a question that pops up from time-to-time on online forums such as the nigh-dead Irish Language Forum[^2] or Reddit[^3]<sup>,</sup>[^4], which is the best place to consult if you're looking for the unqualified speculation of the most annoying people who have ever lived.

My personal advice to those seeking to avoid Christian-isms in their speech is that the great lengths to which they go to avoid Christian-isms in their Irish speech would be better spent avoiding Christian-isms in their daily English speech:
I would be greatly surprised to learn that any of these individuals who are so greatly opposed to saying <span class="gaelic">Dia duit</span> go to similar lengths to avoid terms like "Goodbye", "Bless you", or exclamations such as "Jesus Christ", "Oh my God", "Gadzooks", or similar.
